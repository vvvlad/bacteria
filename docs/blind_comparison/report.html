Motivation & Initial Concerns

The question was straightforward: given two rich microscopy datasets (control and CAM-treated spheroplasts), can an LLM discover the meaningful biological differences without any domain hints?

Before starting, three concerns shaped the experimental design:

Concern 1: Rediscovery cost. A blind analysis will inevitably spend effort confirming the obvious (CAM cells die faster, lose more fluorescence). Every minute spent rediscovering known facts is a minute not spent finding something new. The risk is a thorough but uninformative report.
Concern 2: Domain-dependent metrics. Many of the most informative comparisons require biological knowledge to even formulate. "Compute F(t)/F(0) including all cells until they disappear" is not a generic statistical operation — it encodes the understanding that cell disappearance is itself informative. Similarly, stratifying swelling by cell fate requires knowing that fate is biologically meaningful. A blind pass is likely to miss these.
Concern 3: Spurious patterns. With 60+ statistical tests across dozens of metrics, some "significant" results will be noise. Without domain context to weight findings by biological plausibility, the output risks burying real signals in a forest of p-values.

These concerns motivated a two-step design: let the blind analysis run first, then compare its findings against the expert analysis to identify gaps, and fill them with a targeted follow-up.

Two-Step Method

1 Blind Statistical Comparison

An analysis agent received only the CSV file paths and column names. It was told to call them "Condition A" and "Condition B" with no biological context. Instructions: compare every metric, use Mann-Whitney U / KS / chi-squared tests, apply Benjamini-Hochberg correction, report effect sizes. Do not speculate about mechanisms.

Result: 61 tests performed, 36 significant after FDR correction. 12 large effect sizes, 5 medium, 16 small.

2 Targeted Follow-Up

Findings from Step 1 were compared against a pre-existing expert analysis (PPT presentation). Gaps were identified and a second analysis agent was dispatched to fill them: F(t)/F(0) computed correctly for all cells, fate-stratified swelling, SA:V temporal dynamics, CV/nNRM trajectories, lifetime distributions, and temporal correlations.

Conclusions

On the method

The initial concerns were validated:

  • Rediscovery cost was real but acceptable. The blind pass spent most of its 61 tests confirming known differences, but this also served as independent validation and correctly identified the priority ordering (fluorescence heterogeneity as the dominant signal).
  • Domain-dependent metrics were indeed missed. F(t)/F(0) for all cells, fate-stratified swelling, and temporal trajectories all required a second pass informed by biological reasoning. A purely blind approach would have underestimated the fluorescence difference by 2x and missed the fate–swelling interaction entirely.
  • The two-step design justified itself. Step 1 recovered 3/8 expert findings fully and partially found 4 more. Step 2 closed all gaps and surfaced 6 novel findings. Neither step alone would have produced a complete picture.

On the biology

The data paint a consistent picture across both analyses:

  1. CAM and control cells start morphologically identical (area, volume, SA:V all indistinguishable at frame 0) but differ profoundly in intracellular fluorescence distribution from the very first frame.
  2. CAM cells die faster (89% vs 78%), sooner (median 14 vs 18 frames), and more uniformly (lifetime std 4.1 vs 6.5 frames).
  3. The fluorescence heterogeneity signal is the strongest differentiator (5 metrics with |d| > 1.1), stronger than any morphological or kinetic metric. CAM cells begin with highly concentrated fluorescence (high CV, nNRM, skewness) that progressively disperses, converging toward control levels.
  4. Survival rules differ between conditions. In control, bigger cells survive longer. In CAM, initial size is irrelevant; instead, fluorescence heterogeneity (nNRM) is the dominant fate predictor. The CV–lifetime correlation even flips sign.
  5. Swelling is protective in both conditions (survivors swell more than disappeared cells), but the protective gap is 2x larger in CAM, and the overall swelling amplitude is lower in CAM.
  6. Swelling and fluorescence loss are tightly coupled (ρ ≈ −0.51) regardless of condition — suggesting a shared mechanical relationship rather than a treatment-specific effect.
Bottom line. A blind LLM analysis recovered the key findings but needed domain-informed follow-up to get the magnitudes right and surface the most biologically interesting comparisons. The two-step approach — blind first, targeted second — produced both independent validation of known results and genuinely new leads (CV–lifetime sign flip, size–fate decoupling, feature importance shift) that were not in the original expert analysis.
